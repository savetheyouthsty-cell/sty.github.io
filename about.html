About Us

Who We Are

Save the Youth is a student-led cultural exchange organization that connects young people across borders through one-on-one conversation.

Our Story

Johann Kim is the founder and president of Save the Youth, a student-led 501(c)(3) nonprofit that connects American high school students with students in South Korea through one-on-one cultural conversations. He founded it while still a student at Carter G. Woodson High School in Fairfax, Virginia. Save the Youth pairs U.S. Hosts with Korean Partners in grades 7 through 12 for weekly 50-minute sessions on Google Meet. In each session, two students who would never otherwise meet talk about school, family, food, friendship, and the small daily details that make their lives different.

The idea began with Johann’s own move across the Pacific. He spent his elementary and middle school years in Korea, and when he came to the United States, he started high school in an English Language Development (ELD) class. The room was full of students from different countries, and he soon realized that language was only part of the distance between them. Each student carried a different way of thinking: how to speak to a teacher, what counted as polite, what was funny, and what it meant to work hard. Most misunderstandings did not come from bad intentions. They came from not knowing how the other person saw the world.

Johann also learned how much one person can change that experience. During his first months at Woodson, a classmate named Abraham chose to be his partner in PE class. It was a simple gesture, but it made a new school feel less foreign. Abraham became his closest friend, and today he serves as Save the Youth’s Director.

Those experiences raised a question Johann could not set aside. Why should cultural understanding depend on luck, on happening to sit next to the right person? Students learn about other countries from textbooks and screens, yet rarely have the chance to simply talk with someone their own age who grew up somewhere else.

That question led to Save the Youth.

The program’s purpose became clear in one of its early sessions. An American Host and a Korean Partner began comparing what they did after school, and both were surprised by how differently their days were shaped. Neither student was right or wrong. They were curious, and they kept asking questions. That moment of surprise, followed by curiosity, is what Save the Youth hopes to create in every session.

Because sometimes, the best way to understand the world is to sit across from one person and ask how their day went.

What We Believe

We believe that real conversation between young people can break down cultural barriers in a way no textbook can. We believe that curiosity grows when students meet one another as equals, with each side both teaching and learning. And we believe that these experiences should be open to every student who wants to look beyond their own corner of the world.

Where We Are Now

What began as a handful of pairs has grown into a youth-led team of six officers who recruit Hosts, match students, follow each pair’s progress, and share the program with new communities. Teachers in Korea help connect interested students, and American Hosts join from high schools across the region. Our Fall 2026 session runs for six weeks beginning October 19. As we grow, we plan to connect students from more countries.

Our Nonprofit Status

Save the Youth is a registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization recognized by the Internal Revenue Service. Our EIN is 42-2146690. Every dollar we receive goes directly toward running and expanding our programs.
